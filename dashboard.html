Space Optimization Dashboard

This Space Optimization dashboard turns CAIT's transit parts inventory into three lenses: what's on hand today, how lean replenishment policies could get without hurting service, and which items deserve priority in a cycle count. Data shown here is illustrative, built to demonstrate what's possible with CAIT's analytics.

Definitions

Current Inventory Status
Movement rate

Power BI classifies inventory as Active (up to 180 days since usage), Slow Moving (over 180 through 730 days), or Obsolete (over 730 days). The obsolete segment appears only when its count is positive. Percentages use the sum of category-level distinct counts; an item classified in multiple categories contributes to each.

Slotting priority

A score indicating how urgently an item should be placed for easy access, based on pick frequency — shown here crossed with each item's PO risk level.

Storage type

The physical storage class assigned to an item — Pallets, Drawers, Boneyard, and others — based on handling needs.

Supply continuity risk

Whether current on-hand inventory is likely to cover demand until the next delivery, or is at risk of stockout first.

Procurement exposure

Price × procurement lead time × purchase order quantity, summed by item. This lead-time-weighted exposure score is not a dollar value. Larger scores indicate greater combined price, quantity, and replenishment-time exposure.

How to Read This Tab

Current Inventory Status
  1. 1The gauge shows the overall share of inventory that's active (green) vs. slow-moving (red), across all tracked items.
  2. 2Search any item code, description, or priority level in the search box to filter the priority × PO risk breakdown, the storage-by-priority bars, and the item list all at once.
  3. 3The priority × PO risk breakdown shades each cell by how many items fall into it — darker means more items — with row and column totals on the edges.
  4. 4Switch between the High/Medium/Low tabs on the storage breakdown to see where each tier's inventory physically lives.
  5. 5Service availability shows the share of items where on-hand stock may not last until the next delivery, across all tracked items.
  6. 6Use the dropdowns on the item list for a second layer of filtering by priority, storage type, or PO risk level. The colored strip on each card reflects PO risk (red/yellow/green).
  7. 7Click a bar on the exposure leaderboard to filter the breakdown, storage bars, and item cards to that item; click it again (or clear the search box) to reset.

Definitions

Min-Max Optimization
NIVR (Net Inventory Value Reduction)

Estimated total capital freed up when shifting from current policy to the selected min-max policy — shown in the KPI row above.

Lead Time Variability

Simulated vendor supply delay parameters (Normal, 15% Delay, 30% Delay) used to stress-test safety stock thresholds.

Demand Profile

A simulated demand pattern (Stable, Moderate, Spiky) representing how predictable or volatile item usage is expected to be.

Policy Type

The replenishment strategy being modeled — Balanced, High Service, or Lean — each trading off inventory cost against service level.

Fill Rate

The share of demand met from on-hand stock without a stockout — shown as the dial in the Policy Impact visual, colored red/amber/green by how close it is to full coverage.

Inventory Value Reduction

The dollar drop in required inventory value for a specific item under the selected Policy Type. Click an item below to see its detail.

Stockout-Day Risk

How many days, on average, an item's on-hand stock is expected to run out before the next delivery under the selected scenario.

Min-Max Recommendation

Current min/max thresholds per item, alongside the recommended min/max under the Balanced policy.

How to Read This Tab

Min-Max Optimization
  1. 1The KPI cards summarize the optimization model as a whole: how many parts were evaluated, the total capital that could be freed up (NIVR), and how many recommendations were validated.
  2. 2Use the Lead Time, Demand Profile, and Policy Type toggles below to pick a scenario.
  3. 3The Policy Impact dials compare today's fill rate and inventory value against what the chosen scenario would produce. Each dial is colored red/amber/green by how close that policy gets to full demand coverage.
  4. 4The Inventory Value Reduction leaderboard ranks the items driving the largest savings under the selected Policy Type. Click an item to see its detail card below, cross-referenced with its priority, storage, and PO risk from the Current Inventory Status tab; click it again to clear.

Definitions

Cycle Count Optimization
Coverage Target

The % of total exposure you want captured before the cutoff line is drawn. Drag the dial to see the selected item count and item list respond in real time.

Priority Rank & Coverage Curve

Each item's rank toward the cutoff, plotted against how much cumulative exposure is captured moving down the ranked list. The pin marks the model's own suggested "knee" — the point past which adding more items stops paying off.

Selected Items Deck

The items currently inside the cutoff for the scenario and coverage target you've chosen.

Scenario Shift (vs. Baseline)

How the selected item set changes when you switch away from Baseline at the same coverage target — which items are shared, newly pulled in, or dropped.

Recommended Min/Max

The cycle-count threshold recommendation for each selected item — current range vs. optimized range.

Scenario Exposure

The mandatory exposure each scenario is built around — its own real KPI value and label, pulled straight from Power BI (annual movement, inventory value, lead-time exposure, or service-risk coverage, depending on scenario).

How to Read This Tab

Cycle Count Optimization
  1. 1Pick a scenario chip, then drag the Coverage Target dial — every visual below updates together.
  2. 2The Coverage Curve plots Priority Rank against cumulative exposure captured. The moving dot shows your current cutoff; the pin shows the algorithm's suggested knee point.
  3. 3The Selected Items Deck lists every item currently inside the cutoff, ranked highest-priority first.
  4. 4Scenario Shift compares your current scenario's selected items to Baseline at the same coverage target, so you can see exactly what a scenario adds or removes.
  5. 5The Recommended Min/Max list shows, for each selected item, how its cycle-count range would change under the optimized policy.
  6. 6The Constraint Threshold strip at the bottom shows what each scenario is actually optimizing against — Baseline has none, since it isn't constrained.
